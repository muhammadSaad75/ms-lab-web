"How much will my app cost?" is the first question almost every founder asks, and the honest answer is "it depends." That is not very helpful on its own. This article explains *what* it depends on, so you can understand any quote you receive and decide where to spend and where to save.

## The biggest cost drivers

### 1. Number of screens and features

Cost mostly comes down to how much needs to be built. Every screen needs to be designed, built, connected to data and tested. A simple app with sign-in, a list, a detail screen and a profile costs far less than one with chat, payments, maps and an admin panel.

**Tip:** Write down every feature, then mark each one as *must have for launch* or *can wait*. Most first versions can drop a third of the list.

### 2. Backend and admin panel

Many people forget that the app on the phone is only half of the product. If users sign in, save data, or see content that changes, you need a backend: a server, a database and an API. If your team needs to manage users or content, you also need an admin panel. These can cost as much as the app itself.

Ready-made backends such as Firebase can reduce this cost for simpler apps. Custom backends make sense when you have complex business logic or specific data requirements.

### 3. Third-party integrations

Payments, SMS, maps, video calls and CRMs each need integration work, testing and error handling. Each integration adds to the budget. Payment integrations usually need the most testing.

### 4. iOS, Android or both

Building two separate native apps roughly doubles the effort. Cross-platform frameworks such as **React Native** let one codebase run on both iOS and Android, which saves a large part of the build and maintenance cost for most apps.

### 5. Design

Using a clean, standard design system is much cheaper than fully custom illustrations, animations and branding. Custom design is worth it when your brand *is* the product. Otherwise, start simple and polish later.

### 6. Ongoing costs after launch

The launch is not the end of the budget. Plan for:

- **Hosting and services**: servers, databases, SMS and email providers.
- **Store fees**: Apple charges a yearly developer fee, and Google charges a one-time fee.
- **Maintenance**: new iOS and Android versions, library updates and bug fixes.

A common rule of thumb is to budget a percentage of the original build cost each year for maintenance and small improvements.

## How to reduce the cost without cutting quality

1. **Start with a focused first version (MVP).** Launch the smallest version that solves the core problem, then improve based on real user feedback.
2. **Use cross-platform development.** One React Native codebase for iOS and Android.
3. **Use proven services** for authentication, payments and notifications instead of building them from scratch.
4. **Agree on scope in writing.** A clear feature list prevents surprise costs halfway through.
5. **Choose a team that thinks about maintenance.** Clean, typed code costs less to change later.

## Why quotes vary so much

Two quotes for "the same app" can differ a lot because they are often not for the same app. One may include a backend, admin panel, testing and store release, and another may not. When comparing quotes, ask each team for:

- The exact list of screens and features included.
- Whether the backend and admin panel are included.
- Who owns the source code and store accounts.
- What happens after launch: bug fixes, updates and support.

## Getting an accurate estimate

The fastest way to get a real number is a short conversation about your idea, users and must-have features. With that, a team can give you a clear scope and a fixed quote for the first release.
